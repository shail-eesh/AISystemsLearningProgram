### T31 · Autograd engine

Scalar `Value` and NumPy `Tensor`, both reverse-mode, plus `nn` (Linear/Sequential/MLP, three losses),
`optim` (SGD+momentum, Adam), and a central-difference `gradcheck`. Five-step ladder, **61 tests**.

| check | result |
|---|---|
| scalar gradcheck, 30 random graphs | worst rel error **3.6e-10** |
| tensor gradcheck, 10 shape-mixing cases / 147 entries | worst **7.8e-08** |
| whole-MLP gradcheck | worst **8.7e-09** |
| loss curve vs an independently hand-derived network, 200 Adam steps | max divergence **6.1e-17** |
| engine cost vs hand-written gradients | **3.7×** |
| scalar engine vs tensor engine, one 64×8→16 layer | **498×** (27,777 graph nodes) |

Bug found and fixed by gradcheck during the build: `bce_with_logits` built `|z|` as a detached
constant, which computes the right forward value and silently zeroes that term's gradient. Now
`z * sign(z)` with the sign detached, and pinned by a test. Written up in `NOTES.md` and in the
episode-3 script, because it is the archetypal autodiff bug.

### T16A · Matmul kernel (CPU)

Five C kernels compiled on demand through ctypes (`-O3 -march=native -fopenmp`, identical flags for
all five — handicapping the naive one would be a better number and a worse lesson). Six-step ladder,
**36 tests** including odd shapes (37×53×41, 65×33×129) that exercise every blocking tail.

| n=1024 | ms | GFLOP/s | vs naive |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| naive i-j-k | 4098 | 0.5 | 1× |
| i-k-j (one swapped loop) | 259 | 8.3 | 15.8× |
| blocked, single thread | 181 | 12.2 | **22.6×** (capsule asks ≥10×) |
| blocked + 2 threads | 125 | 17.2 | 32.8× |
| blocked + 4-row register tile | 115 | 18.7 | 35.6× |
| NumPy / OpenBLAS | 16 | 135.0 | 256× |

Machine peak computed by hand: 2 cores × 2.10 GHz × 32 FLOPs/cycle (AVX-512) ≈ 134 GFLOP/s.
OpenBLAS measures 135, i.e. ~100% of peak; our best is 14% of it, and `NOTES.md` accounts for the
gap (C not held in registers, no panel packing, no hand-written assembly). Block sweep is flat —
worst/best across six tilings is 1.27× — and the bandwidth-vs-working-set curve shows the cliff
between 1 MB and 4 MB that explains the winning tile size.

### T45A · Softmax & online softmax (CPU)

naive → stable → two-pass fused → one-pass online (`SoftmaxState` with an associative `merge`),
plus `logsumexp`, `log_softmax`, fused `cross_entropy` and its gradient. Five-step ladder,
**49 tests**.

On a 64×128 adversarial grid (logits in ±1e4, plus all-800 and all-−1e4 rows):

| dtype | naive breaks on | online vs two-pass | online vs float64 ref |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| float64 | 64/64 rows | **0.0** | 0.0 |
| float32 | 64/64 rows | **0.0** | 6.4e-09 |
| float16 | 64/64 rows | **0.0** | 4.9e-05 |

Online is **bit-identical** to two-pass in every dtype, and across chunk sizes 1 … 512 … whole row.
Counterweight, also measured and stated in the docs and the script: on a CPU with NumPy it is
**1.8× slower** (4.04 ms vs 2.29 ms on 256×4096 f32). Its value is tileability, which pays in T45B
and T7, not speed here.

### T30 · Tokenizer (BPE) → FinTok

Byte-level BPE with incremental pair counts + a pair→words index (3,233 merges over ~1 MB in ~1 s),
GPT-2 and finance pre-tokenizers, six special tokens, save/load, `truncate()` for matched-size
comparisons. Six-step ladder, **54 tests**.

- **Round trip on 100,000 fuzzed strings: 0 failures** (ASCII, CJK, Devanagari, emoji ZWJ, control
  bytes, all 256 latin-1 bytes).
- **Domain advantage, controlled** (same trainer, same pre-tokenizer, both truncated to 997 merges,
  measured on held-out financial text): FinTok **4.528** bytes/token vs GeneralTok **1.658** vs raw
  bytes 1.000 → **2.73×**.
- **Pre-tokenizer contribution, isolated**: finance 4.788 vs gpt2 4.429 bytes/token → **1.08×**.

**Bug the fuzzer found:** the finance pattern used `\d` for its numeric branches while its fallback
excluded `\p{N}`, so superscript digits (U+00B2/B3) matched *no* branch and were **silently deleted**
from the text. Fixed with a `\p{N}+` branch; regression test now runs every latin-1 byte and a 12k
codepoint sweep through both patterns.

## Deviations from the plan, stated

1. **FinTok-16k → FinTok-3.5k.** Asking the trainer for 16,128 merges on the committed corpus yields
   3,233: it is templated synthetic text and runs out of pairs occurring twice. Rather than pad with
   frequency-1 junk the shipped artefact is 3,233 merges + 256 bytes + 6 specials = **3,495 tokens**,
   and `bench/compression.py` publishes the saturation curve (126 KB → 1,450 merges; 951 KB → 3,233;
   2.8 MB → 4,481) that explains it. The same trainer scales on a real filings corpus
   (`FORGE_ALLOW_NETWORK=1`).
2. **tiktoken/GPT-2 as the external benchmark: unavailable.** `tiktoken` installs, but its vocabulary
   host (`openaipublic.blob.core.windows.net`) is proxy-blocked in this sandbox. The bench records
   that and runs the controlled two-corpus comparison instead, which isolates *domain* rather than
   confounding corpus, vocabulary size and pre-tokenizer at once. The `--gpt2` path stays in the
   bench and will light up on a machine with access.
3. **New dependency:** `regex` (added to `pyproject.toml`) — `re` has no `\p{L}` classes.
